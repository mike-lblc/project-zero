# Three Routes for Heat: Conduction, Convection, Radiation

Heat is energy moving from a warmer place to a cooler one, by three routes that usually act together.

## Conduction

Conduction is energy handed along through matter that stays in place. Fast-vibrating atoms and molecules in the warmer region jostle their slower neighbours, and in metals free electrons carry energy too. Nothing flows as a whole; the material itself is the path.

Medium needed: yes, a material medium. It works in solids, liquids and gases in contact, though solids, especially metals, conduct far better than air.

Familiar example: a metal spoon left in a hot drink becomes warm at the handle, which never touched the drink; energy was passed atom to atom along the spoon.

Source: https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/airplane/heat.html

## Convection

Convection is heat carried by the bulk movement of a fluid. Warmed liquid or gas expands, becomes less dense and rises, while cooler, denser fluid sinks to replace it. The energy rides along with the moving fluid instead of being handed from particle to particle.

Medium needed: yes, specifically a fluid, meaning a liquid or gas free to circulate. A rigid solid cannot convect.

Familiar example: the air near the ceiling of a heated room is warmer than near the floor, because air warmed by a radiator rises and spreads out; the warmth is delivered by moving air.

Source: http://hyperphysics.phy-astr.gsu.edu/hbase/thermo/heatra.html

## Thermal radiation

Thermal radiation is energy carried by electromagnetic waves that every object emits because of its temperature. Warmer objects radiate more strongly, and most radiation from ordinary warm objects is invisible infrared. Visible glow appears only at very high temperatures, so an object need not glow to radiate.

Medium needed: none. Radiation crosses empty space and also passes through transparent air.

Familiar example: sunshine warms your face on a cold, still day; the Sun's energy crosses the vacuum of space with no material connection.

Source: https://www.physicsclassroom.com/class/thermalP/Lesson-1/Methods-of-Heat-Transfer

## Comparison

| Mechanism | How energy is transferred | Medium requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conduction | Passed between neighbouring particles (and free electrons) in matter that stays put | A material medium in contact; solids conduct best |
| Convection | Carried by the bulk circulation of a fluid | A liquid or gas free to move |
| Thermal radiation | Carried by electromagnetic waves emitted by warm surfaces | None; works through vacuum and transparent media |

## More than one route at a time

A cup of tea on a table cools by all three routes together: conduction into the table and cup walls, convection as warmed air rises off the surface, and infrared radiation to the cooler room.
